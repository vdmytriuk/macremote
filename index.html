01 · Trackpad

The cursor follows your finger

Smooth acceleration, tap and double tap, two‑finger right click, scrolling, drag by holding. A MacBook trackpad that lives in your pocket.

02 · Keyboard

Your keyboard, your languages

Type on your phone's own keyboard: the text lands on the Mac as is, whatever layout the Mac has. Arrows, Esc, ⌘ shortcuts and sticky modifiers are always one tap away.

03 · Apps

Switch apps with a tap

Running apps as tiles with their icons. Tap to switch, hold to hide or quit. A searchable list of every installed app to launch anything.

04 · Screen

Your Mac on your phone

A live view of the display. Touch to move the cursor to that spot, tap to click, two fingers to scroll. Handy from the couch or another room.

05 · Media

A remote that is always with you

Play, pause and next track in any player, volume with the native HUD, mute and brightness. No more hunting for the Mac in the dark.

Installation, step by step

On the Mac

Open Terminal, paste the command and press Enter. It downloads the latest version, puts MacRemote in Applications and opens it. No macOS warnings.

curl -fsSL https://vdmytriuk.github.io/macremote/install.sh | bash

Right after launch macOS asks for Accessibility: it is needed to move the cursor and press keys. Turn MacRemote on in the list.

A phone icon appears in the menu bar. Optionally enable “Launch at login” in the menu.

Why a command instead of a download

macOS quarantines files downloaded by a browser and blocks apps without a paid Apple Developer signature: you get a “Not Opened” dialog that has to be bypassed in System Settings. The command downloads the same disk image without quarantine, so everything opens right away.

The script is open: read it at the address in the command. It only downloads the image from GitHub Releases, copies the app to Applications and opens it. Nothing else on your Mac is touched.

On the phone or tablet

  1. Make sure the device is on the same Wi‑Fi network as the Mac.
  2. On the Mac: MacRemote menu → “Show QR code…”.
  3. Point the camera at the QR code and open the link in the browser (Safari, Chrome).
  4. Add the page to the home screen: in Safari “Share → Add to Home Screen”, in Chrome “Add to Home screen”. The remote opens as its own app, without an address bar.
  5. The token is stored on the device: no need to scan again.
Requirements
  • macOS 13 Ventura or later, Apple Silicon or Intel.
  • iPhone, iPad, Android phone or tablet with a modern browser.
  • A shared Wi‑Fi network. Guest networks often isolate devices from each other.

Questions

The phone can't see the Mac. What now?

Check that both devices are on the same network and that it is not a guest network. If the Mac firewall is on (System Settings → Network → Firewall), allow incoming connections for MacRemote. Ports 8383 and 8384 are used.

The cursor doesn't move even though everything is connected.

Accessibility is not granted. Open the MacRemote menu: if it shows “Grant Accessibility…”, click it. After an app update the permission sometimes has to be removed and granted again.

The Screen tab shows a hint instead of the picture.

Screen Recording is required: MacRemote menu → “Grant Screen Recording…”, turn MacRemote on in the list and relaunch the app.

Does it work with Android?

Yes. The remote is a web page, so any device with a modern browser works: iPhone, iPad, Android phones and tablets.

Is it safe?

Only someone with the token from the QR code can control the Mac. Traffic never leaves your local network, there is no server and no accounts. On public networks you don't trust, just quit the app.

How do I uninstall?

Menu → “Quit”, then drag MacRemote from Applications to the Bin. If you enabled “Launch at login”, turn it off in the menu first. The token and settings live in ~/Library/Application Support/MacRemote.

How much does it cost?

It is free for now. If a paid version ever appears, the free one is not going anywhere.

How do I update?

MacRemote checks GitHub for new versions a few times a day. When one appears, the menu shows “Update to X…”: confirm, and the app restarts on the new version with permissions kept. Running the install command again does the same.
